\maketitle

This note collects corrections, sharpenings, finite-group results, and data
related to Bedert's paper [B]. Its controlling purpose is verification: every
item below was produced while checking, extending, or reproducing [B]'s
argument, and each is presented with its own verification tier rather than as
one combined new result. Parts II and III contain observations obtained
in the course of checking the argument. They are offered as supplements to
the paper, not as claims about what its author intended to optimize. For a
finite subset $A$ of an Abelian group, [B] calls $a_1+a_2$ a unique sum when
its only ordered representations are $(a_1,a_2)$ and $(a_2,a_1)$.

For $A\subset\ZZ/p\ZZ$, Scheinerman [S, Definitions 3.1--3.2] had earlier
treated the same condition and, writing $\nu(A)$ for the minimum ordered
representation count on $A+A$, stated that $A$ is unique-sum-free exactly
when $\nu(A)\geq3$. Green records that Andrew Granville (personal
communication) and Swastik Kopparty (Harvard 2017 open problems session)
each asked him this prime-cyclic minimum question [G, Problem 27].

\part{Corrections and scope}

\section{A 2-torsion scope issue}

At [B, line 291], the proof says that a set with no unique sum is balanced:
the sum $a+a$ has another representation, which is taken to have distinct
summands. In a group with 2-torsion, however, the other representation may be
another diagonal pair. Consider
\[
 A=\{0,1,2,3,5,6,8\}\subset \ZZ/12\ZZ.
\]
The numbers of unordered representations of the twelve residues are
\[
(2,2,3,2,3,2,3,2,3,2,2,2).
\]
Thus $A$ has no unique sum under [B, line 62]. It is not balanced: neither
$0$ nor $6$ is the midpoint of two distinct members of $A$. The proof of the
general-group lower-bound theorem invokes ``no unique sum implies balanced''
at [B, line 458]. The printed proof therefore needs an odd-order clause at
that step, or a separate argument for groups with 2-torsion. This does not
dispute the theorem itself.

The boundary is sharp among cyclic groups of even order.
\begin{theorem}
For $m\geq0$, there is a set $A\subset\ZZ/(2m)\ZZ$ with no unique sum and
some ordered representation count equal to $2$ if and only if $m\geq6$.
\end{theorem}
For $m\geq6$, one may take
\[
 \{0,\ldots,m\}\setminus\{m-2\}\ \cup\ \{m+2\}.
\]
\tier{kernel-certified}
The family and the exhaustive cases $2m<12$ are checked in
\texttt{CycEven.lean}; \texttt{verify.py} recomputes the $C_{12}$ table.

\section{The {\L}uczak--Schoen upper bound}

{\L}uczak and Schoen [LS, Theorem 2] use the ordered representation count of
[B]. Taking $Q=2$ gives, for $p\geq2^{22}$, a set
$A\subset\ZZ/p\ZZ$ with
\[
 |A|\leq4(\log_2p)^2,\qquad r_A(g)\geq4\quad(g\in A+A).
\]
Hence $m(p)\leq4(\log_2p)^2$. Thus the order of growth in [B, Theorem 5]
already follows from [LS]. Bedert's construction improves the constant and
is more direct. The author has confirmed this observation.
\tier{source-grounded consequence of published theorems}

\section{A redundant factor in Proposition 1}

For an indexed family $Z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)$, let $\Sigma(Z)$ be its set of
subset sums and let $d$ be the largest size of a dissociated index set. Then
\[
 |\Sigma(Z)|\leq
 \#\{I\subseteq[n]:I\text{ is dissociated}\}
 \leq\sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom nj
 \leq\binom{n+d}{d}.
\]
Choose the colexicographically least index set representing each subset sum.
Every set shattered by this family is dissociated, and Pajor's strengthening
of Sauer--Shelah completes the count. This removes the first factor from
$\binom nd\binom{n+d}{d}$ in [B, Proposition 1].
\tier{kernel-certified first two inequalities; finite arithmetic check for the last}
\texttt{SubsetSums.lean} checks the first two inequalities and
\texttt{verify.py} checks the Vandermonde step. The author has explained that
he chose the simpler sufficient bound because the factor does not affect the
asymptotic theorem.

\section{The empty set}

The definition at [B, line 282] makes the empty set balanced, while
Proposition 3 asks for $g\in-B$ for every balanced $B$. The repair is to
require balanced sets to be nonempty in the definition, or in the
propositions that use such an element.
\tier{source-grounded definitional correction}

\part{The general-group question}

\section{The minima three and four}

\begin{theorem}
For every finite Abelian group $G$,
\[
 m(G)=3\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad3\mid|G|.
\]
Moreover, if $4\mid|G|$ and $3\nmid|G|$, then $m(G)=4$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Translate a three-set to $\{0,x,y\}$. If it has no unique sum, the pair
$\{0,x\}$ can collide only with $\{y,y\}$, so $x=2y$. Symmetrically
$y=2x$, whence $3x=0$. Conversely, a coset of a subgroup of order three has
no unique sum. This proves the first assertion by Cauchy's theorem.

If $4\mid|G|$, choose a subgroup $H$ of order four. For $a,b\in H$, choose
$h\in H\setminus\{0,b-a\}$. Then
\[
 (a+h)+(b-h)=a+b
\]
is neither the original nor the swapped pair. Thus $H$ has no unique sum.
The first assertion and the elementary two-point obstruction give the lower
bound when $3\nmid|G|$.
\end{proof}
\tier{proved, elementary; algebraic core kernel-certified}
\texttt{Dcyc.lean} checks the three-set algebra, both order-four groups, and
the necessary $C_{12}$ counterexample to the unguarded four-branch.

\section{Census-52}

\tier{exact computation, DRAT-certified}
All 88 Abelian groups of orders $2$ through $52$, and 26 separately labelled
stress groups through order $221$, match
\[
 m(G)=\min\bigl(\{4:4\mid|G|\}\cup
 \{m(p):p\text{ odd prime},\ p\mid|G|\}\bigr),       \tag{2}
\]
with $C_2$ separately having no nonempty such set. This is 114 exact groups,
not a theorem for all finite Abelian groups. Each of the 114 lower bounds has
a retained DRAT proof and verified checker log; the 113 upper witnesses were
recounted directly. The proofs are not distributed with this note. Their
CNF and proof SHA-256 hashes, paths, group sizes, and outcomes are recorded
in \texttt{mg-census-through-52.json}, whose own SHA-256 is
\[
\texttt{f8d1c7119e5e98f6d97c7bd4bc76e7eeac1060915876196040f6df74f9a073e9}.
\]
The manifest does not record proof byte counts, so no byte-size claim is made
here.
This census tests and extends to noncyclic groups the cyclic conjecture stated
by Scheinerman [S, Conjecture 3.40].

\section{A double-cyclic conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}
For every odd prime $p$,
\[
                         m(C_{2p})=m(C_p).           \tag{3}
\]
\end{conjecture}
This is the $n=2p$ case of Scheinerman's published conjecture
$\mu(n)=\min_{q\mid n,\ q\text{ prime}}\mu(q)$ [S, Conjecture 3.40].
The following evidence and reduction do not prove (3).

First, a DRAT-certified exact search shows that every minimum set in
$C_2\times C_p$ lies in one parity fibre for
$p=3,5,7,11,13,17,19,23,29$. Second, write
\[
A_e=\{x:(e,x)\in A\},\quad B=A_0\cup A_1,\quad
D=A_0\cap A_1.
\]
If $A$ is minimum and $D=\varnothing$, projection is injective and $B$ has
no unique sum. Hence $m(C_p)\leq|B|=|A|$, while embedding a minimum set of
$C_p$ in one fibre gives the reverse inequality.
\tier{proved reduction}
Thus empty overlap at the minimum implies (3). Eliminating nonempty residual
overlap remains open.

The naive statement that projection preserves the property is false. In
$C_2\times C_{11}$, the full double lift of
\[
                 \{0,1,2,4,7\}\subset C_{11}
\]
has no unique sum, although its projection does. This counterexample is
kernel-certified in \texttt{Dcyc.lean}. It is not minimum, so it does not
refute (3). It is affinely equivalent to Scheinerman's example
[S, Section 3.6].

\part{Sharpenings of the prime-cyclic argument}

For comparison, if $\alpha(p)$ denotes the minimum cardinality of a balanced
subset of $\ZZ/p\ZZ$, Nedev proved
\[
             \alpha(p)\geq\log_2p+3-\log_2 3
\]
for every odd prime $p$ [N, Corollary 9]. Every unique-sum-free set in
$\ZZ/p\ZZ$ is balanced, and therefore
\[
             m(p)\geq\alpha(p)\geq\log_2p+3-\log_2 3.
\]
This transfer uses only the inclusion of the unique-sum-free sets among the
balanced sets; it does not identify the two problems. Balancedness constrains
the diagonal sums $2x$, whereas the unique-sum-free condition constrains every
sum in $A+A$. The transferred bound is weaker than the asymptotic lower bound
in [B].
\tier{published theorem plus elementary transfer}

\section{A registered exponent-two lower bound}

Palomar Registry entry PALOMAR-2026-08-18-000003 v1 records Xinjie He's Lean
formalization of a theorem from his own working draft. For all sufficiently
large primes $p$, it proves
\[
                 m(p)\geq c\left(\frac{\log p}{\log\log p}\right)^2
\]
for an absolute constant $c>0$. Together with the published upper bound
$m(p)\ll(\log p)^2$, this determines the exponent of $\log p$ up to lower-order
factors.

\tier{kernel-replayed on our hardware; replay log and axiom prints in the README}
This tier applies to the formal theorem and not to a line-by-line referee review
of the proof. The Palomar entry is a registered formal artifact, not a
peer-reviewed publication. Its source describes the mathematics as the author's
working draft; no separate preprint registration is asserted here. The immutable

\href{https://palomar-registry.org/entry?id=PALOMAR-2026-08-18-000003\&version=1}
{PALOMAR-2026-08-18-000003 v1}.

\section{The subsequent Cao--Yuan preprint}

Cao and Yuan [CY] put $G_p:=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$. For a finite Abelian group
$G$ with $|G|\geq3$, they define $m(G)$ as the minimum cardinality of a subset
$A\subseteq G$, with $|A|\geq2$, having no unique sum. Their main theorem
states:
\begin{theorem}[Cao--Yuan, preprint]
For every prime $p$, every set $A\subseteq G_p$, with $|A|\geq2$ and no
unique sum, satisfies
\[
 |A|\geq c_*\,\log p\,\log\log p.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 m(p)\gg \log p\,\log\log p.
\]
\end{theorem}
Here $c_*=1/40000000010006048640$ in their explicit statement. The source is
arXiv:2608.06728v1, submitted 7 August 2026, and is a preprint. Its source
reports a Lean 4 check, but the arXiv source bundle contains no Lean artifact;
that check has not been independently kernel-verified here.

Their exact theorem and the exact theorem below are overlapping but
incomparable. The former bounds $|A|$ and uses a level-four additive dimension
in its structural proposition; the latter bounds Bedert's
$K(A)=|A|/\dim A$ with a particular constant and additional displayed
hypotheses. Neither exact statement implies the other as stated. For the
derived asymptotic role in bounding $m(p)$, however, the Cao--Yuan preprint's
$\log p\log\log p$ lower bound strictly dominates the bound derived from the
theorem below. No theorem stated in this note is refuted.
\tier{Cao--Yuan: unrefereed preprint; comparison: source-grounded adjudication}

\section{A one-log improvement}

Put
\[
 M=\log_2\log_2p,\qquad K(A)=\frac{|A|}{\dim A}.
\]
Replacing the update
$|S'|\leq\max(2|S|,|S|^3)$ in [B, lines 479--494] by a two-point update
throughout gives the following conclusion under the hypotheses displayed in
the formal statement:
\[
             K(A)>\sqrt{M/404}.                 \tag{1}
\]
This is a genuine asymptotic improvement, replacing the triple logarithm
under the square root by a double logarithm. The free inequality
$K(A)\geq1$ dominates (1) whenever
\[
                         p<2^{\,2^{404}}.
\]

There are two formal statements. The statement in
\texttt{ObjectLayer/O7Headline.lean} is
\tier{conditional, kernel-certified; frontier hypothesis}
conditional on the stated Lev rectification interface \texttt{hBLR}. In that
formalization we do not establish this hypothesis, and nothing in the development supplies an instance of it. The extracted theorem
\texttt{sqrt\_improvement\_from\_isUSF\_internal} has the identical remaining
hypotheses and conclusion but no \texttt{hBLR} binder.
\tier{kernel-certified per the recorded original elaboration; ``unconditional''
only in the absence of the \texttt{hBLR} binder; not freshly re-verifiable on
the present 16 GiB host; import closure and axiom print in the README}
The associated satisfiability check supplies a concrete model of the arithmetic
hypotheses, so the unconditional statement is not vacuous merely by
inconsistent numeric assumptions. The paper itself says that the simplest sufficient
form of this slowly growing factor was chosen and that its exact shape was
not optimized [B, after Theorem 3].
As a contribution to the asymptotic lower bound for $m(p)$, this result is
superseded in role by both later bounds in this note: the Palomar theorem
above already gives the strongest asymptotic exponent, and the Cao--Yuan
preprint [CY] gives an intermediate one. Its exact theorem, formal artifact,
and verification tier are unchanged.

\section{Sharpness of the simultaneous-selector threshold}

Let $f(p)$ be the largest cardinality for which the selector conclusion used
in [B, Lemma 5.1] holds for every $S\subset\ZZ/p\ZZ$. Then
\[
                         f(p)=\lceil\log_2p\rceil.
\]
Equivalently, a selector exists for every $k$-set precisely while
$2^{k-1}<p$. The general upper obstruction is
\[
 \{0\}\cup\{\lceil p/2^i\rceil:1\leq i\leq\lceil\log_2p\rceil\}.
\]
\tier{exhaustive computation at 29 primes across five complete bands, plus a
hand proof of the general upper bound with machine-checked algebraic
identities; not kernel-certified}
The exact computations cover all primes in the five bands from $(4,8)$
through $(64,128)$. In particular, all $234{,}531{,}275$ seven-subsets pass
at $p=127$, while an eight-subset fails. The rectification threshold used
here originates in Lev's Theorem 1 [L]. The gain over the displayed
$2^{|S|}\leq p$ hypothesis is exactly one element, not a change of scale.
